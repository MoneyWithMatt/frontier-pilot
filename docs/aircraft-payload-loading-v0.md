## SDK 1.2.4 findings

The installed SDK at `C:\Users\mattr\Desktop\MSFS 2024 SDK` was inspected read-only. `PAYLOAD STATION COUNT`, `PAYLOAD STATION NAME:n`, and writable `PAYLOAD STATION WEIGHT:n` expose up to 15 indexed stations; payload writes use pounds and are converted explicitly to the economy's canonical kilograms. MSFS does not expose dependable authoritative station maxima, arms, or a maximum-payload value for every default and third-party aircraft. Missing limits remain labelled unavailable and are never inferred from default weights.

`EMPTY WEIGHT`, `MAX GROSS WEIGHT`, `TOTAL WEIGHT`, `CG PERCENT`, `CG FWD LIMIT`, and `CG AFT LIMIT` provide factual values when the aircraft publishes them. The desired-state path refuses a reported station, maximum-payload, or maximum-gross violation. Planned CG remains unavailable when the simulator does not expose sufficient station geometry; current simulator CG and published limits remain visible.

Fuel uses reported tank quantity/capacity, `FUEL WEIGHT PER GALLON`, and indexed engine-combustion state. Writes use US gallons and require every detected engine to be off. Modern or third-party fuel systems may not enumerate through the eleven legacy named tank SimVars; unknown engine or tank data fails closed.

## Live Weight & Balance diagnostics

The Weight & Balance tab is a read-only diagnostic view of the current simulator state. **Refresh from MSFS** uses the existing loading adapter and shows the observation timestamp. It does not use the stock EFB planning cache, the cargo manifest, or Frontier Pilot's planned-load calculations for its primary values.

The displayed values come directly from these SimVars:

- `TOTAL WEIGHT` in pounds, converted to kilograms only for dual-unit display;
- `CG PERCENT` in `percent over 100`;
- `CG FWD LIMIT` in `percent over 100`;
- `CG AFT LIMIT` in `percent over 100`;
- `PAYLOAD STATION COUNT`, followed by `PAYLOAD STATION NAME:n` and `PAYLOAD STATION WEIGHT:n` in pounds for every reported one-based station index.

The adapter converts the three CG fractions to displayed percentage points. These are direct simulator reports, not Frontier Pilot CG estimates. Unsupported values are shown as unavailable. Payload station diagnostics deliberately show raw MSFS station weights; unlike the editable Payload tab, they do not subtract Frontier-managed cargo to derive a non-Frontier baseline.

The station model is aircraft-generic. Count, names, indices and weights are discovered on each read and writes address the supplied station indices. No PA-28 station number, name, baggage compartment or station limit is hard-coded in the runtime loading path. The SDK and current validation cap the station count/index range at 15. The existing legacy named-fuel-tank list is an aircraft compatibility limitation for fuel only. Station arms, structural limits and an authoritative maximum-payload value remain unavailable when an aircraft does not publish them.

The Black Square Duke should therefore expose its own forward/rear stations and accept the same indexed payload writes if its add-on systems allow standard SimConnect payload changes to persist. That expectation still requires the forward-versus-rear live test; third-party aircraft can overwrite standard payload state or present a separate cached EFB model.

The first Duke test on 2026-09-17 showed correct gross-weight and CG movement. Its Rear Baggage station contained 18.143694758 kg (exactly 40 lb) before Frontier cargo was assigned. The Duke package defines this as payload station 8 and implements its own saved-payload local variables. Frontier Pilot therefore records the observed 40 lb as other-aircraft payload and preserves it: setting the station's Frontier cargo allocation to zero removes only the Frontier delta and returns the station to 40 lb. The Payload tab now labels this column **Other payload** and the resulting combined station value **Total after**. Removing or changing other-aircraft payload remains the responsibility of the aircraft/EFB; Frontier Pilot does not silently claim or erase it.

Matt completed the first full Black Square Turbine Duke cargo flight on 2026-09-17. Persistent records show the 100 kg Arandis shipment loaded in the Duke, airborne and movement evidence, 116.65 NM maximum distance from Eros, verified arrival, complete physical unload, delivered inventory, and one 200 TPC ledger payment. This is the first live acceptance of the full MSFS-integrated load-flight-unload-payment loop on that aircraft.

A subsequent PA28 video showed a separate add-on compatibility behavior: the committed 120 kg station load was present while seated, then the PA28 reported the managed station as empty after entering walkaround. The loading adapter requests the explicit SimConnect `USER_AIRCRAFT` proxy; it does not read `USER_AVATAR` or `USER_CURRENT`, so this was not an avatar/aircraft selection error in Frontier Pilot. The PA28 or its add-on loading logic is rebuilding the standard station state during the control-mode transition. Frontier Pilot correctly retains the authoritative cargo manifest and reports the simulator cargo as missing. Restoration remains explicit and is never performed on connection or refresh.

## Desired-state cargo architecture

The Aircraft Loading page is a general aircraft state editor. A nonterminal accepted shipment can make reserved cargo available at its origin, but the mission does not own the station editor. Terminal Completed, Cancelled, Forfeited, or skipped jobs no longer present cargo as available. A nonterminal manifest can still show Frontier-managed cargo physically aboard after a job forfeiture.

Each station plan stores its inferred non-Frontier baseline and desired Frontier allocation. The target is always `baseline + desired Frontier cargo`; changing 100 kg to 40 kg is a verified -60 kg physical delta and never removes the baseline. Desired cargo may range from zero through the shipment quantity remaining after verified destination deliveries. Cargo can be loaded in multiple verified steps, such as 0 -> 60 -> 100 kg. Additional cargo is permitted only at the shipment origin and never beyond actual reserved availability.

Aircraft-loading schema 5 can aggregate several explicitly selected shipments when their origin and destination match. The station plan remains one physical desired payload, while immutable manifest legs retain each job/shipment identity, quantity, loaded amount and delivered amount. Economy transitions, flight attempts, delivery evidence and settlement remain separate per shipment. Existing manifests migrate as one-leg loads.

At the verified destination, a negative delta adds only the physically removed quantity to destination inventory. Partial delivery is recorded without payment. When the complete shipment quantity has been delivered, the tracker-supplied immutable evidence completes the existing atomic inventory/job/payment transaction exactly once. Away from the origin and destination, the UI requires an explicit forfeiture confirmation before any write. Confirmation physically applies the requested reduction, records its coordinates, terminates the job and shipment without payment, and prevents later completion. Unloaded reserved cargo that never left the origin is released through an inventory-audit transaction.

Some aircraft, including the tested PA-28, can reset payload stations when switching between the cockpit and walking avatar. The committed manifest remains authoritative for Frontier cargo. If MSFS reports the recorded non-Frontier baseline after exit, an explicit zero-cargo plan at a verified destination treats the physical removal as already present, verifies that baseline, and commits delivery without restoring and removing the same cargo again. Merely detecting missing simulator payload never creates delivery or payment.

The economy format is schema 5. It adds explicit loaded, delivered and forfeited shipment quantities plus an idempotent cargo-transfer log. Existing schema 1-4 states migrate atomically: historical Loaded/InTransit quantities remain aboard, historical Delivered quantities remain delivered, all identifiers and ledger entries are preserved, and corrupt/future documents remain untouched. Aircraft-loading storage is schema 4. It retains shipment totals, delivered quantities, desired-state intent, transfer-command recovery data, boarded verification data, and the explicit reset checkpoint described below.

## Apply, verification and recovery

For positive loading in the production workflow, the outside action follows `fresh MSFS read -> validate avatar/location/access/limits -> persist exact plan and authorization`. It deliberately does not write payload while the player is outside. After the player boards, the same primary UI action performs `persist seated intent -> write -> read-back -> retained-state read-back -> atomic economy transfer -> commit manifest`. This prevents aircraft such as the PA28 from clearing a successful outside write during the walkaround-to-cockpit transition. Negative cargo deltas continue through the guarded outside apply path because unloading requires avatar presence. The retained-state read occurs after a configurable three-second delay. Replaying an intent reuses the same command and cannot add cargo, inventory, or payment twice.

Reconciliation evaluates the stations containing Frontier cargo independently. A pilot, passenger or add-on changing an unrelated station cannot hide missing cargo in the assigned station. The load summary separately displays authoritative shipment cargo and cargo currently detected in MSFS. Missing cargo is never restored automatically; the guarded restore command writes only the managed stations and must also pass retained-state verification.

An all-zero desired state also provides a bounded recovery path for a failed **uncommitted** loading intent. It is available only when the economy still records the shipment as Reserved with zero loaded quantity and the flight attempt has no airborne, movement, arrival, or delivery evidence. Frontier Pilot then returns stations touched by that intent to their recorded pre-intent baselines, preserves the current live value of every unrelated station, verifies the MSFS result, removes the stale flight attempt, and records a reusable `ResetAtOrigin` manifest checkpoint. The shipment and source inventory remain reserved, and no inventory-audit or financial-ledger entry is created. The same shipment can then qualify and load again. This path cannot reset committed cargo or completed delivery state.

The live Station 5 failure was not caused by a station-index or kilogram/pound conversion error. The saved manifest recorded a verified 100 kg target on Station 5 with a 0 kg non-Frontier baseline. A later MSFS read reported Station 5 back at 0 kg and reconciliation correctly classified the cargo as missing. A subsequent PA28 test established the timing: MSFS returned 120 kg immediately after the Station 5 write, but later returned zero; its Weight and Balance panel was exactly 264.6 lb short. This is consistent with the aircraft or simulator loading system overwriting the external write. The retained-state check detects the short-lived acceptance before inventory is committed. The former dedicated unload code nevertheless subtracted the saved cargo from the live 0 kg, causing “Station 5 no longer contains enough weight.” The desired-state path treats that live 0 kg as the physical baseline for a requested zero state, verifies that nothing needs subtracting, and then commits the inventory consequence. Future mismatches report station index, inferred baseline, managed allocation, expected target and observed read-back.

The dedicated mission-specific unload button is no longer the primary UI. The Payload tab now has one context-sensitive primary control. It reads **Confirm loading plan** outside, becomes **Board aircraft to continue** while waiting, and reads **Apply cargo to aircraft** after boarding. Reducing station values, including **Set cargo to zero**, uses the same control for unloading. Missing committed cargo presents **Authorize cargo restoration**, then follows the same boarded apply step. Recovery mechanics no longer occupy permanent toolbar buttons. Fuel remains on its own tab. Automatic distribution and new simulated loading were removed from the UI; existing simulated manifests remain readable and retain their explicit compatibility recovery path.

The loading screen is sized for the application's 850 x 650 minimum window. The station table uses compact columns, the cargo summary shows only available, aboard, requested and detected quantities, and the current factual blocker appears beside the action. Repeated introductory and authorization text was removed. Persistence paths, reconciliation state and connection detail remain available under the collapsed **Technical details** section. Fuel reconciliation choices appear only while that decision is required.

Typed station values are editor drafts and every valid edit immediately updates the coordinator's authoritative requested plan. **Set cargo to zero** updates all rows as one plan before action eligibility is evaluated. Live telemetry and qualification refreshes update factual aircraft readings without replacing an unfinished draft with the committed manifest. The summary displays total job cargo, cargo available to load at the current location, cargo aboard, the requested aboard amount and cargo already delivered. At the origin, loading moves quantity from available to aboard; at the destination, unloading moves quantity from aboard to delivered. Cargo can be physically unloaded elsewhere only through the explicit forfeiture confirmation.

## Other retained corrections

New flight attempts persist `min(10, max(0.5, directRouteDistanceNm * 0.5))`. Existing schema-1/schema-2 attempts retain their historical 10 NM requirement. Loading records `Loaded`; InTransit begins on proven departure, while all airborne, movement, arrival, identity and stationary thresholds remain unchanged.

### Pending manifest selection recovery — 2026-09-22

The next live refusal reported that the authorized shipment was no longer active, although its economy job was still Accepted. Startup restored the pending manifest but Jobs rebuilt its loading selection only from Loaded/InTransit shipments. A Reserved shipment with a pending intent was therefore absent from the selection; the boarded write and physical verification could succeed before the coordinator discovered the missing UI context.

Boarded application repeats this validation before simulator writes and captures the matching job for its commit. Missing or terminal jobs fail without a write; recovery never accepts a different shipment as a substitute. This uses the existing Jobs service and flight tracker, with no save migration or manual modification of player state.
